Reading. Under full knowledge of both placements an attacker needs two holders per victim; with a secret second hash the holders per victim needed rise with the residual uncertainty c, roughly one informed holder per candidate for a certain downgrade. Rekeying restarts every flow resident in table B, so its benign cost is large at short periods (table above). How fast a static key could be learned is not simulated here: an assumed one bit per refusal probe gives about 11 probes for a small keyed polynomial space and about 15 per victim for a full linear key (`docs/lit/adaptive_arms_design.md`, hand-derived), which puts learning time at seconds; a rekey period that beats that would violate the benign-cost limit. Not modelled: probe crafting, moving victims, adaptive holders, hardware cost of rotation. Emulator abstraction, one day, 10 draws.

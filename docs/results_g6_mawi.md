## H-D5 equivalence and the D4s control (primary day, f = 10%)

E_D5 / E_D4 = 0.934, 90% interval [0.906, 0.964] over 30 draws; pre-registered equivalence bounds [0.9, 1.1]: **inside**.

D4 against D4s (2-way set-associative): (E_D4s − E_D4) / E_und = +0.251 [+0.241, +0.261] over 30 draws. An interval that excludes zero means independent hashes matter; if it includes zero, the claim is two-choice at equal capacity, not independent hashes.

**Pre-registered prediction "D4s within ±0.1 of D4 in recovery": FALSIFIED** (R_D4 = +0.76, R_D4s = +0.50, gap +0.25). Post-hoc, untested explanation: the two ways of one set both fail when the set holds two holders, about twice the rate of two independent slots (2ρ² against ρ²), which would roughly double the residual excess. Independent hashes therefore matter for the load balance against an oblivious fill; this says nothing about robustness against a hash-knowing attacker.

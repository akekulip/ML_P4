# Adaptive and probing attacker arms for D4/D5 (design by the sdn-networks-expert agent, 2026-09-24)

Hand-derived predictions, nothing was run when this was written. Key points kept here; the arms actually run are in `docs/preregistration.md` (G6 addendum 4) and `docs/results_g6_adaptive.md`.

- The constant factor of two cannot be erased: with full placement knowledge the attacker holds two slots per victim. Information can only erase what is above two, the extra cost of the secret second hash.
- The real question is a race: the attacker's learning time against the rekey period, and the benign cost of rekeying against the M1 limit (0.5 percentage points of downgraded-packet share).
- The pilot's 12 to 15% for a secret second hash is probably a floor from benign occupancy of table B, not attacker skill (a random table-B holder hits the victim's slot with probability about 1/32,768). A control with the first table held and no informed table-B holder tests this.
- A linear (CRC-style) hash only exposes the victims' own rank: for 20 victims at most about 300 key bits matter, not the full 1,560.
- **Leaked-bits arm.** A leak of b bits leaves c(b) = min(2^(k−b), N/2) candidate table-B slots per victim (k about 11 for a keyed degree-15 polynomial, about 300 for 20 victims under a full matrix). With m holders on distinct candidates, the victim's downgrade share is about S0 + (1 − S0)·m/c; holders per victim for 90% excess ≈ 1 + 0.9·c, capped by N/(2V).
- **Probes.** Assuming at most one bit per refusal probe, a keyed polynomial (about 2,182 irreducible degree-15 choices) needs about 11 probes at one bit each; earlier estimates of 20 to 35 imply about 0.3 to 0.55 bit per probe. Filling table B to about 50% first costs about 22.7 thousand holders. Wall time is seconds, not minutes. A static polynomial key should be treated as public against an adaptive adversary.
- **Rekeying.** Rotating the second hash every T seconds restarts every flow resident in table B, a mass downgrade whose cost falls as 1/T. If learning time is about 10 s there may be no T that both defeats learning and stays within M1; that conflict is the finding.
- **Moving victims, adaptive holders:** protected time ≈ 1 − g_gap·s·e^(−τ/Δ); rotating holders dilute an age rule by the handover gap (about 268 ms), leaving a factor g/(T_rot + g).
- Claim a reviewer would accept: two holders per victim under full knowledge; more only while the second hash is secret, falling by about half per leaked bit; a static polynomial key is treated as learnable; second-hash secrecy is credited only over a rekey interval whose benign cost is stated against 0.5 pp. Not claimed: crafting feasibility, hardware cost of rotation, other traces, or protection beyond the constant factor against a hash-knowing adversary.

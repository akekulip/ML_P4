## PI memo: eight reframings, ranked
Top 2 (novelty x testability x venue fit, both scored 100): **(1) self-downgrade evasion** — an attacker forces its own malicious flow onto the weak fallback (1 holder unkeyed, 2 under D4c), turning small mean accuracy harm into a targeted evasion result on PeerRush's botnet classes; cheapest test: rerun `g6_targeted.py` with victims = malicious-labelled flows, compare full/fallback/targeted recall. **(2) identity aliasing as an integrity attack** — the XOR-fold hash lets a crafted tuple alias a victim's slot AND tag, corrupting features rather than displacing them (a different, likely worse, attack class); cheapest test: add an `xor_fold` hash mode, enumerate alias classes, replay with merged alias packets, measure verdict flips. Also ranked: (3) spatial two-choice across two switches instead of a second hash, (6) per-prefix slot quotas with a real (non-constant-factor) guarantee, (3)+(4) a mean-field phase-diagram theory with a parametric workload generator, (5) a price-of-a-slot frontier (formalizes why D1/D3/rent failed), (7) classifier-aware admission by fallback margin, (8) a workload generator as a support tool. PI recommends starting with (1), auditing (2) in parallel.

## SDN-networks-expert memo: seven new mechanisms, ranked
**Top 2 to test first:** **A. lease plus verdict memo ("classify then release")** — freeze a phase-k verdict into the existing memo table so the slot becomes takeable once a flow is provisionally classified, attacking the high-load failure directly; predicted R about 0.9+ against a non-rotating attacker, 0.1-0.3 against a rotating one (so the rotating-holder arm is the real test); Tofino cost about 0 stages in a controller-table variant. **B. off-path spill with verdict return** — refused flows get mirrored to a software classifier whose verdict is written back to the memo; hash-independent, gives a ceiling for any slot-side scheme (predicted R 0.9+ at all loads with unlimited capacity); near-zero switch cost. Also ranked: C. partial-key cuckoo one-kick relocation (refusal falls toward rho^4; stage-1 is already full so low confidence it fits), D. per-ingress-port admission budget (immune to hash knowledge, needs a non-spoofable resource), G. contention-adaptive gate threshold (fixes benign-only contention, not the attack), H. pinned reservations from controller priors (good for OT's static session sets, near-zero coverage on MAWI), F. tiered mid-accuracy model on collision-tolerant sketch features. Rejected: random replacement (zero-sum), rekeying (already falsified by G6 addendum 4).

## Research-scientist memo: quantitative findings plus seven methodological ideas
**Two findings computed for free from existing tables (not new runs):** (A) the D4s "2 rho^2 vs rho^2" explanation is quantitatively close (bucket-of-2 formula gives a ratio of 1.75-1.9 against the observed 2.03); (B) the iso-refusal attacker-cost multiple of 3.7x overstates cost at iso-*harm*: comparing excess at equal absolute harm gives a multiple closer to 2.4-2.5x, about the same as doubling the table would cost. **Top idea (1): an instrumented mechanistic model** fit on already-run sweeps, with a concrete miss: the naive marginal law over-predicts recovery at high load by 0.03 (low load) growing to 0.2-0.6 (high load), always in the same direction — a real mechanism gap (candidate: holders get a higher admission rate under two-choice than benign newcomers do, or effective occupancy is undercounted). Also ranked: (2) a phase-diagram figure with theory contours, (3) new metrics (flow-time on the accurate path, downgrade half-life, accuracy-at-risk), (4) variance-reduction (control variates, common random numbers, an influence-function estimator) to fix the PeerRush marginal power problem, (5) a causal 2x2 with a placebo attacker and a d=3 dose-response arm, (6) a parametric benign-workload generator, (7) packet-concentration and a labelled-to-unlabelled "transfer coefficient" so MAWI's flow counts get an implied accuracy prediction.

# Downgrade attacks on stateful in-network classifiers: status report

Date: 2026-09-24. Author: akekulip. Repository: `ML_P4`.

This report covers the paper track in `src/dgrade/`. The earlier MVM-Lite class project (in-network decision-tree inference on Tofino-1) lives in `mvm_lite/` and is finished. Every number below comes from a results document in `docs/`, each generated by a committed script from logged runs. Pre-registered hypotheses, deviations and post-hoc additions are in `docs/preregistration.md`; additions decided after results were seen are labelled there and here as exploratory.

## 1. Question

(This is track 2 of the repository; the class project is track 1, `mvm_lite/`. See the top-level `README.md`.)

NetBeacon-style in-network classifiers give their accurate per-flow model only to flows that win a hash-indexed storage slot (65,536 direct-mapped slots). Flows that lose a slot are answered by a weaker per-packet model. NetBeacon's authors argue this fallback is harmless and do not measure it. We measure the accuracy cost, whether a sender that fills slots can force it on third-party flows, and whether a defence helps.

## 2. Method and limits of the evidence

- A packet-level emulator of NetBeacon's flow-slot logic (`src/dgrade/netbeacon_sim.py`); the table evaluator agrees with the shipped tables on every checked packet. Everything below is **emulator output, not switch output**.
- Benign traces: PeerRush (labelled, one capture) and two MAWI days (headers only, 120 s slices, one capture each). Draws vary the clock start and the holder placement on a fixed capture, so intervals over draws do not measure variation between captures. A benign-flow bootstrap is reported beside them.
- The attacker is an analytical abstraction: long "holder" flows sending one packet per 250 ms on uniformly random slots. It is oblivious, not adaptive, and does not probe. The targeted pilot in section 3.4 gives it slot knowledge; nothing models hash search, probing or recirculation races.
- MAWI has no labels, so its loss is benign flows downgraded (oracle flow-size gate unless stated). PeerRush loss is macro-F1 against the capture labels on an 8,192-slot table sized to match MAWI's benign load.
- Every contended run is paired with an isolated reference or a no-attack run at the same clock start.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 The downgrade is real, concentrated and small in accuracy terms (G1 to G5)

- About 1% of PeerRush flows lose their slot at 65,536 slots; the full-versus-fallback gap on them is bimodal, driven by a few very large flows and by the clock start.
- On MAWI the flow-size gate matters a great deal: with the shipped PeerRush-trained gate 8.8 to 10.9% of flows and 26 to 33% of packets are downgraded; with an oracle gate 0.03 to 0.05% of flows and 1.6 to 3.2% of packets.
- On PeerRush (8,192 slots) the table already loses 0.0058 macro-F1 with no attack (30 draws). A fill at 10% nominal holder load adds +0.0065 (30 paired draws; draw interval [+0.0039, +0.0090], benign-flow interval [+0.0008, +0.0121]); at 25% it adds +0.0144 (30 draws; `docs/results_g6_pr.md`). Against an isolated macro-F1 of 0.843 the harm is resolved and small.

### 3.2 Defences that did not work (G1b, G4a, G5)

- **A salt is a no-op** (CRC is affine over GF(2); identical outcomes in 30 of 30 draws). **A secret irreducible polynomial** leaves the fill excess unchanged (keyed over unkeyed 1.00 to 1.02).
- **D1, the wrap fix, cuts benign harm but makes fills worse on PeerRush.** It lowers benign downgrades with no attack (MAWI −0.20 to −0.34 points; PeerRush macro-F1 +0.0020). Under a 10% fill on PeerRush the excess rises from +0.0062 to +0.0221 (4 clock starts) and pre-registered recovery is negative (−0.88 at 10%, −0.60 at 25%). Removing the wrap window alone causes this (`docs/results_g4a_d1parts.md`); on MAWI the same change is near-neutral (recovery 0.03, mitigation +0.01 in both days). The two datasets are not reconciled: a measured difference is that without the wrap window the collision-refusal share on PeerRush varies between draws (2.5 to 9.4%, against 1.5 to 1.9% with it), consistent with heavy benign flows being blocked for long stretches when a holder lands on their slot; that is an observation, not a tested mechanism. D1's hardware cost and switch equivalence are unverified, so D1 is not called free.
- **Rate-based rent (D3)** fails: MAWI flow-weighted recovery 0.11 to 0.18; on PeerRush it costs 0.0100 to 0.0108 macro-F1 with no attack (upper bound over the 0.01 limit) and has negative recovery. The median benign long flow sends 2.7 to 3.7 packets/s, below the holder's 4, so a rate threshold cannot separate them.
- **The value rules** were killed by their pre-registered gate (G5-0): forcing agree-so-far flows onto the fallback costs 0.0368 macro-F1 after phase 4 and 0.0128 after phase 8, against a limit of 0.01.

### 3.3 Two-choice admission (D4, D5): the first defence that passes its rules, with a bounded range (G6)

**Design.** The same total capacity is split into two half-size tables with independent hashes (table A the shipped CRC32, table B a second irreducible-polynomial CRC). A packet uses the slot where its flow is resident; a newcomer takes a takeable candidate and is refused only when both are held. D5 keys the second hash per draw. Multi-choice hashing is an established flow-table technique (HashFlow, HashPipe, cuckoo variants); the contribution here is its measured effect on classifier accuracy under slot holding. The literature check was shallow (`docs/lit/overnight_literature.md`). Flowrest's public full-version P4 (`third_party/Flowrest/P4/Full_version/unibs_flowrest.p4`, lines 223 to 233) computes one CRC32 flow id and one CRC16 register index and shows no second candidate slot, so it does not already implement D4, but it advertises low-collision flow management and belongs in related work with Heracles (NDSS 2026) and SketchFeature (NDSS 2025).

**Pre-registered results (MAWI, oracle gate, f = 10% nominal load, `docs/results_g6_mawi.md`).**
- Recovery of the attack's excess downgraded flows R = 0.76 (draw interval [0.74, 0.77]; benign-flow interval [0.75, 0.77]; 30 draws) against a model prediction of about 0.78; replication day 0.78 (10 draws). Weighted by packets instead of flows (large flows count in proportion to their traffic) R = 0.80 (interval [0.65, 0.89]). Collision-refusal share under fill falls from 1.70% to 0.08%. Benign gain (no attack) is reported separately: 572 to 51 downgraded flows.
- At 25% load R = 0.55 (primary) and 0.58 (replication); at 50% load R = 0.17. Matched-refusal attacker cost 3.7 times the holders (lower bound 3.5).
- D5 is equivalent to D4 on fills: E_D5 / E_D4 = 0.934, 90% interval [0.906, 0.964], inside the pre-registered [0.9, 1.1].
- M1 (no attack) passes on both days (downgraded-packet share falls by 1.5 and 3.0 points).
- **PeerRush accuracy (8,192 slots).** No-attack cost is negative (D4 is better by 0.0036 macro-F1). Under fill D4 cuts the excess from +0.0065 to +0.0009 at 10% and from +0.0144 to +0.0022 at 25%. At 25% the draw-level recovery is 0.85 (interval [0.63, 1.04]), which would be a strong pass, but the benign-flow interval for mitigation ([−0.0025, +0.0274]) includes zero, and the pre-registered demotion rule makes the verdict a **weak pass**; a stricter reading of the rule would call it inconclusive. At 10% the PeerRush result is descriptive only (marginal power). The controls on PeerRush (10 draws each): D5 matches D4 (R = 0.89 at 10% and cost −0.0042 with no attack); D4s is not clearly distinguishable from D4 on accuracy (R = 0.74 at 10% and 25% against 0.85, intervals overlap), although its collision-refusal share is higher (1.07% against 0.35% at 25%), so the MAWI D4s gap is not confirmed on PeerRush accuracy.
- Under the pre-registered defence-level rule (mechanism strong pass, accuracy at least weak pass, M1, M3) D4 passes, **as an emulator result on an oblivious attacker**.

**Two free post-hoc checks (not pre-registered).** The D4s recovery gap has a closer quantitative account than "roughly double": a bucket-of-2 collision model gives a ratio of 1.75 to 1.87 over the measured occupancy range, close to the observed 2.03. Separately, comparing at matched *harm* rather than matched *refusal*, an attacker needs only about 2.5x the load (not the 3.7x matched-refusal multiple) to reach D4's harm at f = 25% starting from the undefended harm at f = 10% (655 against 677 downgraded flows) — at low load D4 and doubling the table cost the attacker similarly, and D4's real advantage is that doubling does not compile.

**Failed prediction (MAWI).** D4s (the same-index 2-way set-associative control) was predicted to match D4 within 0.1; it recovers 0.50 (primary) and 0.52 (replication) against 0.76 and 0.78, so the prediction is **falsified** and independent hashes do help the load balance under an oblivious fill. An untested post-hoc explanation: two ways of one set fail together when a set holds two holders, about twice the rate of two independent slots. This says nothing about robustness against a hash-knowing attacker.

**Where it stops working (post-review, exploratory).**
- *Benign load.* At a fixed nominal load of 10% per slot, recovery falls with table size (benign occupancy 6 to 7%: 0.75; 11 to 13%: 0.57; 22 to 27%: 0.07; 45 to 54%: −0.68).
- *Attacker budget.* At a fixed number of holders (6,554), recovery is 0.75, 0.42, −0.35 and −0.77 as the table shrinks; at 8,192 slots D4 leaves slightly more downgraded flows under attack than the undefended table (6,030 against 5,565). D4 does not protect a table when the attacker holds a large share of it.
- *Shipped gate.* With the shipped flow-size gate (about a million flows competing) D4 still gives R = 0.72 on 10 draws (collision-refusal share 3.44% to 0.47%); this arm is not directly comparable with the oracle-gate load sweep.
- *Recovery is a ratio against a baseline that D4 itself lowers.* In the absolute count D4 leaves fewer downgraded flows under attack than the undefended table in every oracle-gate row except the fixed-budget 8,192-slot row.
- *Placement.* Putting a newcomer in table A whenever it is takeable (D4a) fails M1 (+0.54 points of downgraded packets against a limit of 0.5) and lowers recovery to 0.65. Preferring a never-claimed slot and otherwise table A (D4c, needs only a tag-zero check, no timestamp comparison) matches D4 (R = 0.77 primary, 0.79 replication; M1 passes). The hardware cost of any placement policy is unverified.
- *Larger table instead.* The same attack on a table of twice the size leaves 643 downgraded flows (primary; 527 on the replication day) against 212 and 158 for D4 at the original size; doubling costs SRAM, D4 costs a second hash and a register split (`docs/results_g6_mawi.md`, exploratory baseline). On the compiler, the doubled table does not fit at all (15 stages, section 3.3 Deployability), so the resource-matched alternative is the original 65,536-slot NetBeacon, which is the undefended arm.
- *Targeted pilot* (`docs/results_g6_targeted.md`, exploratory): with the victims' slots given to the attacker, one holder per victim downgrades 100% of victims in a one-table design, 15 to 16% under D4 (the victim lands in its other slot), and two holders per victim are needed to reach 100%; with a secret second hash and two holders, 12 to 15%. This is a constant factor of two against a hash-knowing attacker. It does not model probing (a small keyed space can be learned in about 20 to 35 refusal probes; `docs/lit/overnight_adversary_model.md`), adaptive holders or recirculation races.
- *Rent on top of D4 (exploratory, addendum 3, MAWI primary day only, 10 draws).* D4 on the fixed clock of D1 (D4d1) raises recovery at 10% load to 0.87 (D4c 0.77) and adding age-only rent eviction (D4age) to 0.91, both with no-attack cost that passes M1; at 32,768 slots with a fixed 6,554 holders D4age reaches 0.63 against D4c's 0.44. At 16,384 and 8,192 slots D4age does not help (−0.34 against −0.33, and −0.89 against −0.78), so under the pre-registered rule it is **not shown to be helpful**; rent does not repair D4 when the attacker holds a large share of the table. Two predictions failed: D4d1 was expected to be worse than D4c under attack (on PeerRush D1's clock was worse; on MAWI it is better), and D4age was expected to help at 8,192 slots but not at 65,536 (the reverse happened). These arms were not run on PeerRush, where D1's clock hurt, so no claim is made about them beyond MAWI.
- *Leaked key bits and rekeying (exploratory, addendum 4; `docs/results_g6_adaptive.md`).* If the attacker knows which c table-B slots could hold a victim (a key leak), downgrade scales with the share of candidates it holds (50% at m/c = 0.5, against 57% predicted; with the whole candidate set held 85 to 89% of victims fall, not the predicted 100%, so the pre-registered closed form is partly falsified) and the control floor is 15%. Rekeying the second hash restarts every flow resident in table B: every tested period exceeds the 0.5-point benign-cost limit (+1.96 points at 5 s down to +0.59 at 60 s; a 120 s slice cannot test longer periods). With an assumed one bit per refusal probe a small keyed space is learnable in seconds (hand-derived, not simulated), so a static second-hash key should be treated as learnable and the dependable guarantee against a hash-aware attacker is the constant factor of two. Not modelled: probing procedures, moving victims, adaptive holders.
- *Second-hash independence* passes on all three tuple sets (joint collision rate over the product of marginals 0.99 to 1.02; `docs/results_g6_hash_check.md`).

**Fidelity gate (G8): the compiled-semantics abstraction does not change the headline claims, within the tested range.** A second emulator (`src/dgrade/netbeacon_tofino.py`) reproduces what the compiled D4c program does that the abstract emulator does not (an XOR-folded hash message, a delayed recirculated write with the races that follow, the wrapped clock). At delta = 0 with sorted hashes it matched the abstract emulator bit for bit on saved MAWI and PeerRush outputs (the hard gate). With the fold hash on, MAWI recovery is 0.76 against the abstract 0.77 (|diff| 0.01, gate 0.05) and PeerRush mitigation is +0.0124 against +0.0122 (|diff| 0.0002, gate 0.004): both pre-registered agreement gates passed. A delay sweep to 1,000 microseconds left recovery flat (0.78 to 0.79) even as race events (re-initialisation, feature contamination) grew into the thousands; no dual-owner event was observed in this sweep. This validates the abstract emulator's D4c results against a closer approximation of the compiled program, but not against the switch: recirculation delays above 1 ms, aliasing rates above what these two captures show, and dual ownership under real contention remain untested (`docs/results_g8_compiled_semantics.md`).

**Deployability: compile-feasible on the local SDE 9.13.1 toolchain, not yet validated on the real 9.13.2 toolchain, the software model, or the chip.** D4c, in the variant that splits only the identity registers per way (`p4/dgrade_d4c/`), compiles with the local SDE 9.13.1 compiler in 12 stages with 0 errors, at +1 stateful ALU, +5 SRAM blocks, +58 hash bits and +3 hash-distribution units over unmodified NetBeacon; a doubled-table NetBeacon (131,072 slots) does not compile (15 stages), so doubling is not an alternative on this hardware (`docs/results_d4c_compile.md`). **This is compile-feasibility, not deployment**: the switch runs SDE 9.13.2, and the compile does not run the design, so the 9.13.2 toolchain, the software model and the chip are untested, and the compiled P4 differs from the emulator in ways that matter (recirculation delay and a both-ways-owned state, 17-bit tags in way A, the wrapped clock, and an XOR-fold symmetric hash whose aliasing touches 0.01 to 0.29% of tuples). A follow-up compile study (offline, same local compiler; not run on the switch) found three cheap additions that also compile: moving the per-packet tree earlier frees one stage (critical path 11 to 10); the unwrapped (D1) clock then costs 0 extra resources, settling D1's hardware cost for the compiler (behaviour on the model is still owed); a keyed, additively-combined identity tag (not XOR, which stays linear and reproduces the alias) closes the aliasing weakness at +2 stateful ALUs and +10 SRAM blocks, though its probe has a known unfixed bug (an alias should be forced to a null verdict, not inherit the victim's); combined, these fit in 11 of 12 stages, one fewer than D4c alone (`docs/lit/innovation_memos_2026-09-24.md`). **This combined design ("P13") is a separate, post-hoc implementation study and is not evidence for D4c's pre-registered claims**; D4c, as compiled and evaluated above, remains the paper's evaluated design. The earlier paper analysis that a full two-table split does not fit still stands (`docs/lit/overnight_p4_feasibility.md`).

### 3.4 Detectability of the fill attack (H3, re-analysed with observed alarm rates)

Downgrading half of the benign long flows needs about 82 to 83% nominal holder load (about 56% distinct slots, since holders draw slots with replacement). Per-second bins against thresholds frozen on the primary day: a new-flow-rate detector at p99 stays at baseline; a byte-rate detector rises modestly but transfers badly across days (benign-only alarm rate 9.6% on the replication day); a packet-rate detector alarms in 35% of bins at 10% load and in essentially every bin from 25% on the primary day. Packet rate is itself a volumetric signal, so the attack evades two pre-registered detectors (new flows and bytes) at the thresholds used, not volumetric detection in general. A matched-refusal attacker against D4 needs about 3.7 times the holders, which would raise the packet rate further. Per-/24 detection is not evaluated.

## 4. Deviations, failed predictions and post-hoc changes

- The D4s prediction failed on MAWI (3.3), as did two predictions about D4d1 and D4age (3.3, rent on top of D4); the load sweep fell short of the pre-registered expectation that the benefit would persist until 2·q_b + q_h nears 1.
- The PeerRush accuracy verdict rests on a demotion rule applied as written; the draw-level result alone would be stronger.
- The G4a recovery ratio counted a defence's own benign gain as mitigation; G6 separates them. D1's earlier "1 to 4% recovery" on MAWI is mostly that benign gain.
- Reported intervals use a two-sided 97.5% level, the Holm bound for two comparisons, not for the four pre-registered hypotheses; sign-flip p-values are Monte Carlo and unadjusted.
- After the headline results were seen the following were added as exploratory: benign-flow bootstrap on MAWI, the fixed-budget sweep, the shipped-gate arm, doubled-table and D4s baselines to full draws, D4a and D4c placements, and the targeted pilot (`docs/preregistration.md`, G6 addenda).
- The collision-refusal decomposition by incumbent type, required by the G6 entry, could not be computed: saved outputs do not record who held the slot. Only outcome-code shares are reported.
- The pre-registered check that the shipped flow-size gate admits the holder pattern was run late, after the results: the shipped model scores the holder header pattern as long (100% of flows) and gives every holder packet the per-packet code the emulator assumes (`docs/results_g6_gate_check.md`); it covers one header pattern only.

## 5. Not done

- No switch validation: the compile on the switch host with SDE 9.13.2 and the software-model diff need approval (`docs/sde_validation_request.md`). The offline 9.13.1 compile of D4c exists (3.3); the emulator still assumes instantaneous recirculation and a sorted-tuple hash.
- No adaptive, probing or hash-searching attacker; targeted pilot on one day only.
- G3 (evasion, H4) is blocked on the CICIoT2023 attack folders; the retrained NetBeacon is not written. The Oracle-V ceiling is not run.
- D4d1 and D4age were not run on PeerRush, where D1's clock is harmful.
- **Second accuracy workload attempted and killed (G7):** ISCXVPN2016 (local copy is VPN-only, 29 captures in 16 session groups) was retrained as a NetBeacon-style workload and failed its pre-registered gate: on held-out sessions the full-state path scores macro-F1 0.347 against 0.654 for the per-packet fallback (`docs/results_g7_gates.md`, `docs/results_g7_diagnosis.md`). The accuracy claim therefore still rests on PeerRush alone.
- Evidence rests on three captures (two MAWI slices, one PeerRush).
- A separate finding (the compiled hash's XOR-fold structure lets distinct tuples alias one identity, not just a slot) is tracked outside this evaluation; its falsification test found no classification change at the tested rate (`docs/lit/symmetric_hash_aliasing.md`).

## 6. What the evidence supports

**Framing to use:** D4 is a slot-capacity-neutral mitigation against untargeted flow-state contention (it preserves NetBeacon's 65,536 logical flow slots, at a cost of +5 SRAM blocks, +1 stateful ALU and +58 hash bits, `docs/results_d4c_compile.md`) that improves the availability of the accurate classifier path, with only constant-factor protection once placement is targetable. It is not logic-neutral (a second hash, lookup logic and possibly stages), and it is not a robust defence against downgrade attacks.

- **Supported (emulator, oblivious uniform-slot fill):** splitting a per-flow table into two independently hashed half-size tables reduced the excess benign flows downgraded by 0.76 at 10% nominal holder load (0.55 at 25%) on MAWI, and reduced the PeerRush macro-F1 excess (+0.0144 to +0.0022 at 25% load) with an interval over benign flows that includes zero for the mitigation. The benefit shrinks with benign load and attacker budget and reverses at high load; a same-index control recovers about half; a keyed second hash costs nothing on fills; a cheap placement rule (D4c) keeps the result.
- **Not supported:** that D4 is validated on the real toolchain, the software model or the chip (it is compile-feasible only, on a local 9.13.1 toolchain, against the switch's 9.13.2), that hashing independence adds robustness, protection against hash-knowing, probing or adaptive attackers beyond a constant factor, generalisation beyond three captures, novelty of the mechanism, or any stealth beyond the two volumetric detectors named.
- **Paper framing:** a measurement paper on how access to flow state changes classifier accuracy, with a negative-results core (salt, keyed polynomial, D1, D3 and the value rules fail) and one bounded positive result (two-choice admission). Venue: workshop or measurement track on current evidence; a security venue needs the targeted and adaptive arms with a real attacker model, a resource-matched comparison and switch validation.
